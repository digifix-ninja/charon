# Changelog

All notable changes to Charon are listed here.

## 0.3.0

In Charon 0.3.0 a sync no longer changes your files by itself: Charon shows you the list of changes first, and carries them out only after you confirm it. If you want a job to sync on its own, you can turn on **Full automation** for it, behind two clear warnings that data can be lost. This release also closes the ways Charon 0.2 could lose data that a safety review found, and adds tests for them that run against real folders.

### Nothing changes without your OK

- **The list comes first:** when a job is due (by hand, on change, on a schedule or when its drive is connected), Charon looks at both folders and prepares the list of changes. Looking never changes your files (when the names already in a folder can't show whether its drive tells upper and lower case apart, as in an empty folder, Charon may write a small test file of its own at the top of the folder and remove it at once). The row then says *Needs your OK*, and nothing crosses until you open the list with *Review and sync* and confirm it. A new job opens its first list as soon as you press *Save and review*.
- **A list you can read:** it shows both folders and their drives, what happens on each side, whole folders that would be removed, conflicts, and notes about places left out or not read. You can filter it (*All*, *Removals*, *Replacements*, *Conflicts*, *New*), find a path, and copy it with *Copy list*. When a sync would replace or remove something, the *Confirm* button says so (*Confirm: copy 23, replace 2, remove 3*), and Enter never presses it.
- **Warnings when a list looks wrong:** when a list looks like something went wrong (a large share of one side would be removed or replaced, files would be replaced by empty ones, a folder suddenly looks empty, a folder is on another drive than at the last sync, or files would be gone for good because safe deletes are off), the list warns you, and *Confirm* waits until you tick *I've looked at the removals and replacements*. A list that would remove files on one side while the other side is empty can't be confirmed at all.
- **Exactly what you saw:** confirming carries out that list and nothing else (when it removes a folder, system files inside it such as `.DS_Store` or `Thumbs.db` go into `.charon-trash` with it). A list can't be confirmed once it is more than 15 minutes old, once Charon has noticed a change in the folders, once the job's settings changed or the job synced since, or when a folder is no longer the one the list was made for (another drive under the same name): you look again instead.
- **Checked again before each step:** right before a file is copied, replaced or removed, Charon looks at it once more, on both sides. If it is no longer as it was when the list was made (its size or date changed because you edited it meanwhile, or it came back on the other side), that step is skipped and reported, never forced. When a copy is skipped this way, only the removals on the side it was going to that could be its old copy (the same name, or the same size and date) wait with it. The others go ahead, so one file an app keeps writing to doesn't hold back the rest.
- **Conflicts are settled through a list too:** *Keep source*, *Keep destination* or (for two files) *Keep both* first shows exactly what that choice does, and nothing changes until you confirm. The version that is replaced goes to `.charon-trash`, even when safe deletes are off.
- **Every waiting job in one place:** *Sync all* in the window (*Sync All…* in the menu bar or tray) starts the jobs that run by themselves and opens *Waiting for your OK* for the others; *Review Waiting Jobs…* in the menu bar or tray opens the same sheet. It prepares each waiting job's list at once. *Quit Charon* in the menu bar or tray asks first when changes wait, with *Review now* to open the same sheet. You confirm each job on its own: there is deliberately no "confirm all".
- **Reminders:** with *Notifications* on in Settings, a notification tells you when changes wait, a reminder follows about two hours later, and then one a day while they still wait. None come for paused or offline jobs, or while *Pause everything* is on.
- **Edits wait too:** after you change what a job syncs (its folders, mode, conflict rule, what it leaves out or safe deletes), its next sync waits for your OK. A job can't be edited while it syncs.

### Full automation, with a safety net

- **Off unless you turn it on:** each job has a *Full automation* switch in its *When* step. Two warnings come first: *Full automation can lose data*, then a *Last warning* whose red *Turn on full automation* button only works once you tick *I understand that data can be lost and I accept the risk.* It is meant only for people who know exactly what it does. Such jobs carry a *Full auto* tag.
- **One more OK first:** a job with full automation syncs by itself only after a list you confirmed: its first sync, after you turn the switch on, after you change what it syncs, and after you press *Stop* or pause it while it syncs. When there is nothing to do, you confirm the empty list once. It doesn't sync by itself while its list is being made, or while a list made for it in the last 15 minutes waits for your OK.
- **The switch turns itself off** when you change the mode, turn safe deletes off or swap a Mirror or Backup job's folders, so you read the warnings for the job as it is now.
- **Held back when something looks wrong:** every automatic run makes a fresh list first. If anything in it needs a careful look (one side looks empty, too much would be removed or replaced, files would be replaced by empty ones, a folder is on another drive or has lost Charon's marker, the receiving drive is short of room, or Charon doesn't trust its two-way memory), nothing is touched. The row says *Held for your OK* with the reason, a notification tells you (with *Notifications* on), and the job waits for you.
- **Slow damage counts too:** what automatic runs removed or replaced adds up. A run that would remove or replace something is held when, together with the automatic runs of the last day, that comes to too much of one side, or when, together with everything automatic runs removed or replaced since you last confirmed a list, it comes to more than 20 files and more than a quarter of a side. That longer count only goes back as many days as *Keep deleted files* in Settings (unless that is 0), the time `.charon-trash` keeps the originals. That way damage that spreads slowly, like a broken tool that rewrites a few dozen files an hour, is held back too once it adds up to that much, while the originals are still in `.charon-trash`.
- **Nothing syncs while Charon can't save:** when Charon can't save its jobs and settings (a full disk, say), it syncs nothing, since it couldn't record what a sync did: automatic syncs and *Sync now* don't start, and a list you confirm changes nothing. The window and the tray say *Charon needs your attention*, with the reason under the title bar. Charon tries to save again about once a minute and carries on once it can, but an automatic sync that fell due meanwhile isn't made up: it comes at the job's next due time.
- **Automatic runs never empty the trash:** old files in `.charon-trash` are only cleared by a sync you confirmed whose list said so, so the originals stay there for you.
- **Drives that show changes late:** when an item goes missing on a network share or a drive run by a program (sshfs, rclone and the like, but not a USB disk that Linux reads through ntfs-3g or exfat-fuse), an automatic run removes its copy on the other side only once it has been missing for ten minutes. Until then that removal waits for a later sync: the row shows *Pending*, the run's summary counts the removals left for a later sync, and the activity log says why. Once such removals are due, the job syncs once more by itself, whatever starts its syncs otherwise, and the wait carries over when Charon restarts. A list you confirm is never held back like this.

### Your files are safer while they cross

- **Replacing a file an app is saving:** where the drive can swap two names in one step, the new version is swapped in and the old one checked; if an app saved a newer version at that very moment, it is swapped back. Elsewhere the old version is moved aside and checked before the copy takes its name. Either way, what an app saved is put back or kept in `.charon-trash`, never written over.
- **Removing safely:** a file is moved aside and checked before it goes, so a version saved at the last moment stays. With safe deletes on, a file removed from an APFS drive is cloned into `.charon-trash` and then removed, so an app that has it open doesn't follow it into the trash. Whenever Charon moves one of your items (aside, into `.charon-trash`, or to a conflict copy's name), it never removes the item's name on its own, so a version an app saved a moment before moves along with it, on NFS shares and sshfs too. A sync interrupted right then may leave an empty file dated early 1980 holding a name. Charon treats it like the half-copied files (and, on macOS and Linux, link copies) an interrupted sync leaves under its temporary names: once nothing has written to such a leftover for an hour, the next sync that runs to the end moves it into `.charon-trash`, and the list or the activity log says so.
- **When an item changes kind:** where a folder became a file (or a file a folder) and that change crosses, the old item is removed only right before the new one takes its place, after every other copy. It waits like any other removal when copies to that side fail, and it goes to `.charon-trash` even with safe deletes off, so it is kept until the new item has landed.
- **A trash that keeps watch:** each sync's folder in `.charon-trash` now has its own id and a list of what went in. A file that changed after it went in (an app that followed its open file there) is never emptied, the list tells you where it is, and full automation stays held until you move that file out of `.charon-trash` or delete it. In two-way jobs the replaced version always goes to the trash, even with safe deletes off, and so does the losing version under *Newest wins*.
- **Room to breathe:** before each copy, Charon checks that it won't eat into the last 2% of the receiving drive (at most 2 GB), so your other apps keep some room. On a drive that can't say how much room it has, this check can't be made. On a Mac, space the system can free by itself counts, as it does in Finder. The list warns when a sync won't fit, or when `.charon-trash` takes a large share of its drive. And when a copy to one side fails, that sync keeps its remaining removals on that side for later.
- **Libraries and databases in conflict are settled whole:** a Photos or Final Cut library, a Logic project, a Pages document, a sparse bundle, or an SQLite database with its journal files that changed on both sides of a two-way job becomes one conflict, settled as a whole from the side you choose; nothing inside it moves on its own. A database's files are written next to their targets first, checked again together, and only then take their places. The list asks you to close the app that uses them before you confirm.
- **Written through before it's recorded:** at the end of a sync Charon asks the drive to write its changes through before it notes the sync as done. On Windows and on some network shares this can only be a request, so let a sync finish, and eject the drive, before you unplug it.
- **A sync that didn't finish:** if a two-way sync couldn't be stopped and recorded properly (Charon was forced to quit, crashed or couldn't stop it in time, the computer stopped, or a drive went away), the next look doesn't trust what Charon remembers: it compares both folders afresh and removes nothing. A folder that goes away during any sync stops it there. Whenever a two-way job compares afresh, the list counts the items that are only on one side and get copied to the other, and names those that Charon's record shows on both sides at the last sync, which were most likely deleted on one side since.
- **A drive that comes back without a sync's changes:** before a sync is recorded as done, Charon looks once more at everything it changed. If a copy it made is gone, a version it replaced (or an older one) is back, or an item it removed is back, nothing of that sync is recorded: a drive unplugged for a moment, or a network share whose server restarted, can come back without a sync's last changes. The job then waits for your OK, and a two-way job's next look compares both folders afresh and removes nothing. Editing a file after Charon synced it never counts as lost. But removing or moving a copy the sync just made, or putting back an item it just removed, before the sync is done does count: the sync then goes unrecorded in the same way, and the job waits for your OK. In a two-way job, a file you move or delete during the sync, after its copy was made, isn't counted as synced: the next sync copies it back rather than remove the copy. On Linux 6.8 and later, a drive unplugged and plugged in again counts as another folder, so a sync on it stops and a list made before can't be confirmed.
- **Places Charon can't read are never taken for empty:** nothing in them is synced or removed, on either side. The job's *Couldn't read* tab lists them, with *Leave this folder out*, and on a Mac *Open Privacy settings* when macOS didn't allow access. Another drive or share mounted inside a job's folder is never entered either, so what's on it isn't synced. Items Charon leaves out with a note (sockets, pipes and device files, and on Windows also links, junctions and names Windows can't handle safely) keep their name out of the sync on the other side too: nothing is copied onto them, and a Mirror doesn't remove the destination's item of that name.
- **Files Charon can't read:** a file a look could list but the sync couldn't read (no permission, macOS privacy settings, a mailbox another program holds open) doesn't make the run fail. It joins the job's *Couldn't read* tab, where you can leave it out, and stays there until Charon can read it, even while it keeps changing; after a restart Charon tries it again. Until it is copied or left out, removals on the side it should go to wait, with a note naming it, since a removal there could take the only copy of what it holds (a mailbox renamed since the last sync, say). A run that finds such a file says so once in a notification (with *Notifications* on).
- **Newest wins only when it can tell:** on FAT and exFAT drives, on network shares and drives Charon doesn't recognise, and when the two times are within 2 seconds of each other, whole hours apart, or one went backwards, both versions are kept instead.
- **Quitting and restarting:** quitting during a sync stops it at a safe point first (the window says *Stopping safely…*): a file it was copying stays as it was, and what already crossed can be recorded. Opening Charon a second time brings up the one that's already running, and two copies of Charon never work with the same jobs at once.

### Drives, names and links

- **Symbolic links travel as links** on macOS and Linux, and are never followed: what a link points to isn't copied, so a backup holds the link, not the file. Drives that can't hold links (FAT, exFAT, some network shares) leave them out and say so. A link that can't be made only because its folder can't be written to fails like any other item and is tried again next time; it doesn't make the whole drive count as one that can't hold links. On Windows, links and junctions are left alone.
- **A link in a file's place:** when a file or folder in the source is replaced by a link (often a film moved to another drive, with a link left behind), Mirror and Backup keep the file or folder in the destination with a note, and two-way jobs ask you.
- **One name, one item:** a name written in two Unicode forms (an accented name from an older Mac drive, for example) is one item, never copied twice or removed. Where a drive ignores upper and lower case, *Report.pdf* and *report.pdf* are one item too, and so are *Fıyatlar* and *Fiyatlar* (with the Turkish dotless ı), as Windows drives see them.
- **What you leave out, in every spelling:** where either drive ignores upper and lower case, what a job leaves out ignores it too, for every letter (*Entwürfe* and *ENTWÜRFE*, Greek and Cyrillic names alike), so Charon never writes into a folder you left out under another spelling of its name. A pattern that wouldn't work with case ignored is refused when you save the job. On such drives, a folder of yours named like one of Charon's own in other case (`.Charon-Trash`) is left out on both sides, with a note.
- **What a drive can't hold is left out:** items the other drive can't take (a name or path it refuses, a file too big for it, two names that clash there) are left out with a note. Some of these Charon can tell in advance; others it only finds out by trying, so they fail once and are then left out until the item or the drive changes, or Charon restarts. A name the drive found taken by another item (a short name like *REPORT~1.PDF*) is tried again as soon as that item is gone. The job's *Not synced* tab lists these with the reason, and *Leave this out* stops them being listed.
- **Long paths:** a file whose path fits the drive is never refused because of the temporary name Charon copies it under: where that would be too long next to it, the copy is made a folder up, never above the job's folder. A conflict copy whose full name would be too long gets a shorter one; where none fits, both versions stay as they are, with a note, until you rename or move one of them, and later syncs go on without them. An item whose path in `.charon-trash` would be too long goes in under its own name at the top of that sync's folder there.
- **The right folder on the right drive:** Charon leaves a small marker in a hidden `.charon` folder in each of the job's folders (for Mirror and Backup, in the source only where it can write there) and remembers which drive each folder was on. If a folder is on another drive than at the last sync, or a Mirror or Backup folder has lost its marker or carries another one, the list warns you, *Confirm* waits for your tick, and full automation holds. If a two-way job's folder has lost its marker, carries another sync's marker or an older one (an older copy restored from a backup, for example), the list says so, the job compares both folders afresh and removes nothing, and full automation holds.
- **Cloud files stay in the cloud:** on macOS and Windows, files that are only in the cloud and not downloaded (iCloud Drive, OneDrive, Dropbox) are left alone with a note: Charon doesn't download them, so they aren't synced or backed up until you download them, and their copies on the other side are left alone too. The job's *Couldn't read* tab lists them. Copies, the old versions Charon keeps and comparisons by contents never download a file either: one that went back to the cloud after the look is skipped and reported.
- **Same file, other date:** in two-way jobs, when the dates alone can't tell whether two files of the same size differ, Charon compares their contents, and files that are the same apart from their dates count as in sync. On FAT and exFAT drives a daylight-saving change no longer makes files look edited, and on drives that don't keep the dates Charon sets, Mirror and Backup don't copy the same files again on every sync.
- **Mac, Linux and Windows details:** on a Mac, copies take their Finder tags and resource forks along (a resource fork even onto exFAT and FAT), and `._` files are never synced as files of their own. On Linux, `user.` attributes travel too. On Windows, read-only files and folders and long paths work. Ownership, ACLs, other extended attributes and Windows alternate data streams aren't copied, and a change to only a file's tags or permissions isn't synced on its own: they go along the next time the file is copied. The run's summary says when files arrived without some of their details, and the activity log names them.

### Overlapping folders and other Charons

- **Folders that are one place:** a job whose two folders are the same folder, or one inside the other, can't be saved. Charon checks this again at every look: by the folders themselves, which sees through links and a drive mounted in two places, and, once the job has finished a sync, by its markers in and above them, which also shows a share reachable under two names. Then the list says so and can't be confirmed, and full automation holds. A copy of a job's hidden `.charon` folder (in files restored from a backup, say) is only mentioned, so you can delete it. When Charon can't tell whether it's a copy (it can't leave a test file in either `.charon` folder, or can't read a marker), it treats it as the folder itself until you delete that `.charon` folder.
- **Jobs that would undo each other:** a Mirror job removes what isn't in its source, so a job that writes into the same folder as a Mirror job's destination (or a folder inside or above it) would see its work removed, and a two-way job would carry those removals back. Charon won't save a job that would make such a pair, and *Duplicate* doesn't work on a Mirror job, whose copy would share its destination. Where Charon couldn't see the overlap when the jobs were saved (a link made later, or a drive or share reachable under two names, which Charon finds by the marker the other job left at its last finished sync), or an older Charon saved the jobs, every look warns you: *Confirm* waits for your tick, and full automation holds. You can still edit such a job as long as its folders and mode stay the same.
- **One sync at a time in any folder:** before a sync changes anything, it leaves a small note in the hidden `.charon` folder at the top of each of its folders where it can write (a Mirror or Backup source too), then looks for other syncs' notes in its folders, in the folders above them, and in the folders inside them where the look met a `.charon` folder. So two syncs of Charon 0.3 don't work in the same place at once, whether they are two of your jobs, two copies of Charon with their own jobs on this computer, or Charon on two computers syncing one share. Jobs that share a folder take turns even when one or both of them only read it (two backups of one folder, or a backup of a folder another job backs up into). Charon 0.2 leaves no such notes, so quit it on every computer first. A job that syncs by itself waits (also when *Sync all* started it), and its row says *Waits for “Archive” to finish syncing, then starts by itself* (or names *another Charon*, or *Charon on* the other computer). *Sync now* and *Review and sync* tell you instead that “Archive” is syncing one of these folders right now, so you look again once it's done. A note that hasn't changed for two minutes holds nothing up: a Charon that quit or crashed leaves one behind, but so does a computer that went to sleep mid-sync, so let a sync of a shared folder finish before that computer sleeps.
- **Confirmed, then waiting:** when you confirm a list while another sync works in the job's folders, Charon says *Confirmed. “Photos” syncs as soon as “Archive” is done.* The row says what it waits for, and the sync starts by itself once the other one is done (within seconds when it was another Charon's). The list doesn't go out of date while it waits, and each step is still checked right before it acts; if a folder is no longer the one the list was made for, the list is dropped and the row says why. Until the sync starts, *Stop*, pausing the job (or everything) or editing the job calls it off. The job no longer counts among those waiting for your OK, so *Quit* doesn't ask about it, and quitting Charon drops it too.

### The window

- **Statuses that say what Charon knows:** *Needs your OK* when changes wait, and *Looking* for a job Charon hasn't looked at yet since it started, instead of a guessed *In sync*. After a restart, changes that waited before still show *Changes wait for your OK* until the first look counts them again.
- **Rows that say what happens:** jobs that wait for you have *Review and sync*, jobs that run by themselves have *Sync now* when nothing waits, and the row says what comes next (*looks at 14:00 and asks you*, or *next sync 14:00* for a job that syncs by itself). The *Details* tab says *Looks every hour and asks you* (or *Syncs every hour*). The row shows what waits (*23 changes wait for your OK · 3 removals*) or why a run was held, and flags places that couldn't be read or items that aren't synced. When a run fails, the row says why, or names the items that couldn't be synced.
- **Progress while a list is made:** the window counts the items on both sides, then the files it compares by contents.
- **Details and menus:** the *Details* tab says whether full automation is on and where removed files go, and the job's menu can *Show removed files* (in two-way jobs, *Show removed files on the source* or *on the destination*).
- **Settings:** clearer texts, and keeping deleted files for fewer days asks first. With *Pause everything* on, nothing syncs by itself, while Charon still looks for changes and shows what's waiting.
- **Steadier:** a slow network share no longer freezes the window. If Charon runs into an error of its own during a sync, the job no longer stays on *Syncing*: the run is recorded as failed and the job waits for your OK.
- **Starting up:** Charon starts without a tray icon when the system has none (closing the window then quits), and explains a missing data folder instead of crashing.
- **Notifications and the log:** a job that fails the same way on every run gets one notification about it, until a run of it goes well. Charon writes a log file to your system's log folder.

### If you're coming from 0.2

- **Your jobs move over:** the first time 0.3.0 starts, it takes over your jobs and settings and renames 0.2's `charon.json` to `charon.json.moved-to-0.3`, so an old copy of Charon started by mistake finds no jobs to run.
- **Every job asks first:** all jobs come over with full automation off, and their next sync waits for your OK. Jobs that used to sync by themselves are listed in *What changed in Charon 0.3.0*, each with *Keep asking (recommended)* and *Turn on full automation…*, which leads you through the two warnings. The activity log of each of those jobs notes the change too.
- **Quit the older Charon first:** an older Charon still running next to 0.3.0 would sync your jobs without the new safety checks. Charon notices the older one when that one saves its jobs, which it does after each of its syncs. From then on 0.3.0 syncs nothing, not even a list you confirm, until you reopen it, while the older one goes on syncing until you quit it. The window and the tray say *Charon needs your attention*, and the window says what to do: quit the older Charon from its icon in the menu bar or system tray, then quit and reopen Charon. An older Charon on another computer isn't noticed at all.
- **Two-way jobs compare afresh once:** Charon 0.3 doesn't trust 0.2's memory of the last two-way sync, so each two-way job's first list compares both folders afresh and removes nothing. Files you deleted on one side since the last sync may come back, and files that differ are handled by the job's conflict rule. The list names the items that 0.2's record shows on both sides at the last sync but are now only on one, so you can spot what you deleted since; 0.2's record is used for nothing else.
- **Links now cross:** 0.2 left symbolic links out, so a job's first list may include links that were never synced before.
- **0.2's half-copied files:** files ending in `.charon-partial` may be 0.2's leftovers or your own. Charon names them in the list and only moves them into `.charon-trash` from a list you confirm; if one of them is yours, rename it before you confirm.
- **Coming from 0.1.0 on Linux:** your jobs now move to the new data folder even when the log made that folder first.

## 0.2.0

- **Updates:** Charon asks GitHub at start-up and once a day whether a newer version is out. When one is, an **Update** button glows in the title bar, the menu bar / tray menu offers the download, and a notification says so (once per version). The download opens the installer for your computer. You can also check by hand from About or the tray menu ("Check for Updates…"), or turn the daily check off in Settings.
- **About** now lists the app's website ([charonsync.app](https://charonsync.app)) and GitHub page, and the developer, **stean.DEV**, with its website ([stean.dev](https://stean.dev)) and e-mail ([a.s@stean.dev](mailto:a.s@stean.dev)).

## 0.1.0 — first crossing

- Sync jobs with a source and a destination: **Mirror**, **Backup** and **Two-way**.
- Triggers: manual, on change, every *N* minutes, daily at a time, and when a drive is connected.
- Dry-run preview before syncing; new jobs open it by themselves.
- Safe deletes into `.charon-trash`, cleared after a configurable number of days.
- Two-way conflicts: keep both, newest wins, or ask.

- macOS, Windows and Linux.
